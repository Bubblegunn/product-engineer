Fix check-in reminders that never fired for guests with no time zone

reminderHour looked the guest's time zone up in a two-entry hardcoded table
(Europe/Istanbul, Europe/London). Anything else — including a null timezone —
returned undefined, and `9 - undefined` produced NaN as the send hour.

Replaced the table with the zone database via Intl.DateTimeFormat, which
resolves any IANA zone and follows daylight saving. Guests with a missing or
unresolvable zone now fall back to 09:00 UTC and are marked `fallback: true`
rather than silently producing NaN. Reminder times now carry minutes too, so
half-hour zones such as Asia/Kolkata land on 09:00 local rather than 08:30.

Counts below: `node --test` on this commit, and schedule() run against
data/customers.json (12 guests, the full roster) on 2026-09-05.
  - 4 of 12 guests had a NaN send hour before this change; 0 after.
  - 8 of 12 (all Europe/Istanbul) were unaffected and still resolve to 06:00 UTC.

For the customer:
What changed: Four of the twelve guests were never getting their check-in
reminder at all, because we had no time zone saved for them and the reminder
had no valid time to go out at. All twelve now get one. Guests in a country
that changes its clocks in spring and autumn will also stop getting the
reminder an hour off after a clock change.
Why it matters: A guest who misses the check-in reminder turns up without
knowing how to get in, and that becomes a phone call to the host.
Automation effect: Adding a new country no longer needs a code change — the
reminder time is worked out from the guest's time zone directly, instead of a
hand-maintained list of two cities.

Not shipped: the four guests without a saved time zone get 09:00 UTC, which is
a real time but not necessarily 09:00 where they are. Fixing that properly
means capturing the time zone at booking, which is a change to the booking
flow and outside this fix. The `fallback: true` flag marks exactly those rows
so they can be found and backfilled.
